\begin{execsummary}
The Department of War (DoW) already operates in a continuous environment. Dependencies change hourly, identities drift, and adversaries adapt faster than periodic review cycles. The core problem is not evidence collection. The core problem is converting evidence into decision-grade assurance at mission tempo.

CAF-RA defines a single governed loop for that mission: \textbf{EMIT $\rightarrow$ ENRICH $\rightarrow$ SCORE $\rightarrow$ SURFACE $\rightarrow$ ACT}. The loop is the architecture. It sets responsibility boundaries, contract surfaces, and audit requirements.

CAF-RA enforces three non-negotiables. First, \textbf{authority must be explicit and enforceable} through signed manifests and policy bundles. Second, \textbf{evidence must be portable and replayable} through sealed envelopes and tamper-evident anchoring. Third, \textbf{enforcement must be mediated and attributable} at real change boundaries, not advisory dashboards.

This paper keeps the baseline simple and pushes sophistication into conformance profiles. It defines testable technical positions, a governance overlay, degraded-mode behavior, and an adoption path that starts in shadow mode and graduates to bounded automation. The objective is practical: enable continuous authorization without sacrificing control, attribution, or rollback discipline [1][2][3].
\end{execsummary}

\section{Problem Statement}

DoW governance processes were built for periodic change. DoW operations now run in continuous change. That mismatch produces two bad outcomes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rubber-stamp governance:} controls are documented but not decision-grade in real time.
  \item \textbf{Paralyzing governance:} serialized approvals cannot keep pace with mission tempo.
\end{itemize}

CAF-RA treats assurance as a control-plane function, not a reporting function. The architecture exists because the unit of operational change is now smaller than the unit of human governance. If assurance cannot operate continuously, operators route around it.

\section{Scope and Non-Goals}

\subsection{Scope}

CAF-RA is in scope for:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Enterprise cloud and platform services.
  \item Software factories and delivery pipelines.
  \item Mission runtimes (containers, virtual machines, managed services).
  \item Tactical and edge deployments, including disconnected operation.
  \item Multi-enclave operation and cross-domain posture exchange.
  \item Integration with operations centers, change control, and audit functions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Non-goals}

CAF-RA does not:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Pick vendors.
  \item Mandate one scoring model.
  \item Define mission tactics.
\end{itemize}

For safety-critical or mission-critical effects chains, additional doctrine still applies. CAF keeps the same role in every context: make authority explicit, mediate action, and preserve replay.

\section{Strategic Drivers}

CAF is shaped by constraints, not trends.

\subsection{Acceleration is policy reality}

When the DoW adopts acceleration as an operating posture, architecture must assume high change rates by default. A control surface that cannot keep pace will be bypassed.

\subsection{Decision-grade evidence is scarce}

Evidence volume is high. Decision-grade evidence is not. Raw telemetry is often missing provenance, schema stability, or confidence fields. CAF exists to close that quality gap.

\subsection{Contested and degraded operation is baseline}

CAF assumes partial connectivity, delayed delivery, time skew, sensor failure, and deception attempts. The architecture must degrade safely without collapsing into either optimism or full shutdown.

\subsection{Federation is unavoidable}

DoW environments are federated. CAF therefore enforces a stable policy and evidence surface through contracts and conformance, not one centrally procured toolchain [2].

\section{Architectural Principles}

CAF-RA adopts ten principles.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Producers and enforcers are principals, not tools.} They require identity, attributes, lifecycle controls, and accountability.
  \item \textbf{Centralize policy decisions, distribute enforcement.} Decisioning without enforcement is theater. Enforcement without centralized decisioning is fragmentation [1].
  \item \textbf{Default deny for consequential change.} Permit by explicit authority and tolerance.
  \item \textbf{Change boundaries are explicit.} Systems can compute posture and recommendations, but side effects must pass mediated enforcement points.
  \item \textbf{Evidence and replay are first-class.} Deterministic reconstruction is mandatory for credible continuous authorization.
  \item \textbf{Rollback and containment are routine.} Reversal speed is part of mission tempo.
  \item \textbf{Context is governed data movement.} Context informs decisions but does not grant authority [1].
  \item \textbf{Evaluation is a gate.} Validation outcomes are release and runtime controls, not after-action reports.
  \item \textbf{Confidence is explicit.} Uncertainty drives stricter behavior.
  \item \textbf{Interfaces are government-owned.} Components remain replaceable via stable contracts and conformance tests [3].
\end{enumerate}

\section{CAF as a Governed Loop}

CAF uses two planes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Runtime plane:} where systems and missions actually change.
  \item \textbf{Assurance control plane:} where authority, evidence, posture, and enforcement are mediated and proven.
\end{itemize}

The architecture is one loop.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{p{0.13\textwidth}X}
\textbf{Stage} & \textbf{Purpose and Boundary} \\\hline
EMIT & Make operational reality legible through attributable evidence producers. \\
ENRICH & Transform raw emissions into decision-grade evidence through a security gateway. \\
SCORE & Compute posture with explicit confidence and explainable evidence links. \\
SURFACE & Present drillable decisions and assurance work units for human supervision. \\
ACT & Enforce bounded change and containment at real control boundaries. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\section{Core Vocabulary}

\textbf{Assurance Scope Manifest (ASM).} A signed, versioned artifact that binds \emph{Authority $\times$ Consequence $\times$ Environment $\times$ Uncertainty tolerance}. It defines what actions are allowed, where, and under what confidence constraints [4].

\textbf{Policy bundle.} A signed, versioned set of executable rules for decisions and enforcement points.

\textbf{Evidence envelope.} A sealed wrapper binding producer identity, schema reference, time and clock confidence, handling labels, integrity anchors, and anti-replay fields.

\textbf{Decision record.} A signed record that binds policy hash, ASM hash, evidence references, verdict, confidence, and expiry.

\textbf{Enforcement point (EP).} A boundary where decisions become effects: merge gates, promotion gates, admission gates, segmentation gates, data-access gates, and bounded response automation gates.

\textbf{Proof ledger.} Tamper-evident anchoring for evidence, decisions, and actions, designed for replay and tamper detection.

\textbf{Assurance work unit.} A bounded supervision thread such as a release, incident, or environment baseline.

\section{Technical Positions by Loop Stage}

\subsection{EMIT: make reality legible}

\textbf{EMIT-1: Producers MUST be attributable principals.} Every producer that influences posture or gating must have verifiable identity, credentials, and attributes for ABAC and audit.

\textbf{EMIT-2: Emissions MUST be envelopable.} Internal producer formats may vary, but admitted inputs must be sealable into the standard evidence envelope.

\textbf{EMIT-3: Validation MUST emit evidence.} Scenario-as-code outcomes, drift probes, and detection-fidelity checks are first-class evidence producers.

\subsection{ENRICH: produce decision-grade evidence}

\textbf{ENRICH-1: Ingestion MUST be a gateway.} CAF requires signature checks, schema controls, label enforcement, anti-replay checks, and quarantine paths for misbehaving producers.

\textbf{ENRICH-2: Labeling MUST happen at ingestion.} Cross-domain handling metadata is part of security state, not presentation state.

\textbf{ENRICH-3: Anchoring MUST support replay.} Evidence must be retained and queryable against stable IDs and semantic versions.

\textbf{ENRICH-4: Schema discipline MUST exist.} Versioning and compatibility rules are required implementation contracts.

\subsection{SCORE: compute posture with confidence}

\textbf{SCORE-1: Posture MUST include confidence.} Confidence fields must reflect completeness, provenance, freshness, and consistency.

\textbf{SCORE-2: Posture MUST be explainable to evidence IDs.} Leaders must be able to drill from verdict to causal evidence.

\textbf{SCORE-3: Score MUST NOT grant authority.} Scoring influences decisions; authority remains principals plus signed artifacts [1].

\subsection{SURFACE: make supervision viable}

\textbf{SURFACE-1: Decision surfaces MUST be drillable.} Operators need trend visibility, provenance, and policy traceability.

\textbf{SURFACE-2: Work units MUST be first-class.} Humans supervise bounded units of work, not raw event streams.

\textbf{SURFACE-3: Cross-enclave summaries MUST be artifactized.} Federation should exchange minimized posture packages by default.

\subsection{ACT: enforce bounded effects}

\textbf{ACT-1: No consequential action without a decision record.}

\textbf{ACT-2: Consequence tiers MUST constrain automation.} Higher consequence requires stronger evidence thresholds, tighter rate limits, and stronger approval controls.

\textbf{ACT-3: Enforcement points MUST sit at real boundaries.} If boundaries are not instrumented, delivery routes around governance.

\textbf{ACT-4: Every action MUST emit evidence.} Actions must report principal, scope, effect, and verification result.

\textbf{ACT-5: Verification MUST close the loop.} Containment and rollback require independent effect confirmation.

\section{Governance Overlay}

The loop is the spine. Governance prevents the loop from degrading into a dashboard.

\subsection{Authority artifacts}

CAF requires signed ASMs and signed policy bundles. Tickets and discussion can be evidence, but they are not authority sources. This follows a code-as-policy posture where versioned artifacts and gates carry enforcement weight [3].

\subsection{Overrides and exceptions}

CAF allows exceptions. It does not allow invisible exceptions. Overrides must be scoped, time-bounded, compensating, and replayable. Under pressure, memory and chat logs are not acceptable substitutes for auditable records [1].

\section{Degraded-Mode and Federation}

\subsection{Degraded-mode survivability}

CAF cannot assume perfect connectivity. In degraded mode, CAF tightens authority rather than relaxing it. Local operations proceed only within local, credibly governed boundaries:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Cached manifests and policy bundles.
  \item Local evidence buffering with integrity retention.
  \item Bounded local enforcement and explicit reconciliation rules.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Federation and cross-domain exchange}

CAF composes authority by intersection, not union, across manifests and policy bundles. Cross-domain exchange defaults to posture summaries with strict minimization. Raw evidence movement is exception-based and governed.

\section{Conformance Profiles and Patterns}

Profiles preserve baseline simplicity while allowing higher assurance depth.

\subsection{CAF-Core}

Require the loop and three non-negotiables: explicit authority artifacts, replayable evidence envelopes, and mediated enforcement at real boundaries.

\subsection{CAF-Standard}

Add control inheritance discipline, validation-as-gate, bounded remediation at moderate consequence tiers, and rehearsed degraded-mode behavior.

\subsection{CAF-High Assurance}

Add stronger provenance, stronger integrity anchoring, outlier detection, formal policy coherence checks where feasible, and stricter cross-domain exchange governance.

\subsection{Reusable patterns}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Evidence-first releases:} promotion requires evidence, not calendar timing.
  \item \textbf{Validation-as-gate:} scenario suites produce gating evidence envelopes.
  \item \textbf{Control inheritance as proof:} inherited controls retain lineage and replayability.
  \item \textbf{Bulkhead gateways:} ingress, export, and orchestration boundaries isolate cascades.
  \item \textbf{Assurance work units:} supervision focuses on bounded threads with explicit outcomes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Adoption Path}

CAF should be adopted in phases.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Shadow mode.} Emit, enrich, score, and surface with no enforcement. Prove decision-grade evidence quality.
  \item \textbf{Low-consequence gates.} Implement promotion and admission controls where rollback is cheap.
  \item \textbf{Bounded containment.} Establish constrained remediation with verification.
  \item \textbf{Federated scaling.} Exchange posture summaries and enforce cross-enclave conformance contracts.
\end{enumerate}

Adoption is complete only when governance behavior changes, not when software is installed.

\section{Recommendations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Adopt} CAF as a loop-first architecture and keep the core vocabulary constrained.
  \item \textbf{Require} signed ASMs and policy bundles before any consequential automation.
  \item \textbf{Implement} evidence envelopes, proof-ledger anchoring, and decision records as mandatory contracts.
  \item \textbf{Establish} enforcement points at pipeline, admission, segmentation, and data-access boundaries.
  \item \textbf{Define} confidence-driven escalation thresholds and degraded-mode operating rules.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

CAF-RA is a practical control-plane architecture for continuous assurance at mission tempo. It does not add complexity for its own sake. It limits baseline complexity to one governed loop and uses profiles to scale depth only where mission consequence requires it.

The architecture is successful when it does three things consistently: it keeps authority explicit, it keeps evidence replayable, and it keeps enforcement mediated at real boundaries. When those conditions hold, continuous authorization becomes operationally credible in federated and contested environments [1][2].
